OPTIMISING ARCHITECTURAL DESIGN THROUGH NATURAL RESOURCE UTILISATION

A Study of Natural Lighting, Ventilation and Energy Efficiency in Buildings

Comparative Study & Overall Findings

This study examines how architectural planning can make better use of natural resources. Two different building categories are considered: Dadjanagar Madrasa as an institutional building and a residential building as a domestic example.

Comparison of the Two Buildings

01. Dadjanagar Madrasa

Building type: Institutional

Institutional buildings accommodate many occupants and require suitable daylight and ventilation across classrooms and shared spaces. Thoughtful window placement, suitable openings and effective building orientation can improve indoor conditions.

02. Residential Building

Building type: Residential

Homes require comfortable living spaces throughout the day. Properly positioned windows, cross-ventilation and suitable shading can help bring natural light and fresh air indoors.

Key Comparison

Parameter Dadjanagar Madrasa Residential Building
Natural Lighting Daylight for classrooms and common areas Daylight for living rooms and bedrooms
Ventilation Airflow across occupied institutional spaces Cross-ventilation within individual rooms
Energy Efficiency Potential reduction in daytime lighting and cooling demand Potential reduction in artificial lighting and cooling demand
Design Priority Comfort for multiple occupants Comfort and privacy for residents

Major Findings

Natural Lighting

Correct window size and placement can increase daylight penetration and reduce the need for artificial lighting during suitable daytime hours.

Natural Ventilation

Properly designed openings can encourage natural airflow, improve indoor air circulation and support thermal comfort.

Energy Efficiency

Reducing unnecessary dependence on electric lighting and mechanical cooling can help lower energy demand and environmental impact.

Final Conclusion

The study demonstrates that sustainable architecture is not only about constructing new buildings, but also about designing and improving spaces intelligently.

Although institutional and residential buildings have different requirements, both can benefit from the effective utilisation of natural sunlight and ventilation. By considering building orientation, window placement, airflow and shading, architects can create healthier, more comfortable and energy-efficient environments.
